Reading this carefully: "Not captured" means the graduate wasn't found in Irish employment or social insurance records that year — CSO's own background note is explicit that this is not confirmed emigration; some may be on career breaks, unemployed, or missed by the matching process. It is the closest available proxy for "left the system," not proof of it. Year 1 (mandatory HSE intern year) is near-universal employment; year 2 is the first year graduates are free to pursue further training anywhere, which is where this data shows the sharpest shift. Neither CSO nor HEA publish employer-level data, so nothing here can directly identify HSE employment — Section 5 uses public-sector employment as the closest available proxy. Source: CSO HGO09, downloaded 2026-08-19.

Two different caveats here, not one: the sector chart (left) is "public sector," not literally "HSE" — CSO doesn't publish employer-level data, but for a Medicine graduate in Ireland, public sector overwhelmingly means the HSE hospital system. The occupation chart (right) is a single Census 2022 snapshot, not a trajectory — each cohort is measured at a different number of years post-graduation (2010 cohort ≈ 12 years out, 2021 cohort ≈ 1 year out), and it only covers graduates who responded to Census 2022 and were matched — a much smaller group than the full graduating cohort (e.g. 147–537 people per cohort here, versus 500-700+ actual graduates). It answers "of those we can see, are they still doctors?" (consistently yes, 92–98%), not "of everyone who graduated, what fraction still practise?" Source: CSO HGO14 and HGO16, downloaded 2026-08-19.

RCSI's undergraduate intake is unusual among the six schools — majority non-EU/international every year shown (107 of 189 total UEM entrants in 2023/24), unlike the other five, where Irish/EU students are the majority. Nationally, Irish/EU intake has been rising (767→903) as non-EU intake has fallen (672→523) — plausibly the +200 EU-place expansion agreed between the Department and the medical schools in July 2022 (phased 2022–2026) taking effect, though this data can't confirm that causally on its own. Fee side: Irish/EU UEM students pay no tuition (Free Fees Initiative) plus a €2,500 Student Contribution charge (2025/26); non-EU UEM students pay full fees, roughly €55,000–€61,000/year depending on institution. Source: Dáil written answer, Minister for Further and Higher Education, Research, Innovation and Science, 27 March 2025 (PQ 42), attached data file "Medicine Student Data".

07 What the government pays

State contribution per medicine student, per year — from the Minister's own written Dáil answers, not a single official "cost per student" figure (the Minister said outright in 2021 that none exists).

State funding streams, per student per year

Undergraduate is three separate named streams; GEM is one

Funding streamPer student, per year

These four bars are deliberately not summed into a single "total." The Minister's own 2021 answer names RGAM, the Fottrell top-up, and the Free Fees payment as three distinct funding lines for the same undergraduate place, but doesn't state outright whether a given student's place draws all three simultaneously or whether Fottrell-designated places are funded through that route instead of the standard RGAM rate. Adding all three gives ≈€18,680 — plausible, but not confirmed by the source, so it isn't asserted here. GEM's €14,500 is a single, clean, twice-confirmed figure (phased up from €11,950 in 2021/22 via Budget 2023 and Budget 2024, reaching €14,500 for all places by 2026) — the two funding models aren't structured the same way, so they don't reduce to one comparable number. Source: Dáil written answers, Minister for Further and Higher Education, Research, Innovation and Science — 9 Nov 2021 (PQ 510, RGAM/Fottrell/Free Fees figures), 19 Oct 2023 (PQ 104) and 11 Sep 2023 (PQ 2047) (GEM phasing to €14,500).
