The medical workforce, in seven years

HSE publishes a dense, ~100-page Medical Workforce Report every year — full of real, specific numbers on doctor supply, vacancies, and who's filling the gaps. Almost nobody reads the whole thing. This pulls seven years of it into one page: where things stand right now, and how they got there.

ⓘ Scope note: 2019/20–2025/26 — the years the report consistently covers both NCHDs and Consultants (it was NCHD-only before, under a different title, back to 2010). HSE's own site was down when this was pulled; every figure here traces to a specific table/figure in a specific year's PDF, including one real cross-report data revision — see data/raw/hse/SOURCES.md for exact citations.

04 Irish-qualified — consultants and trainees too, not just NTSDs

Section 03 only covers NTSDs, because that's the only breakdown HSE itself publishes. The Medical Council of Ireland separately publishes where every registered doctor qualified, split by Division of the Register — close proxies for Consultants (Specialist Division) and training-scheme NCHDs (Trainee Specialist Division), not just the General Division doctors closest to HSE's own NTSD category. The share of Irish-qualified doctors fell in all three divisions from 2022 to 2024 — fastest among trainees (80.6% → 71.3%, −9.3pts), well ahead of consultants (74.4% → 72.7%, −1.7pts).

Two important gaps this doesn't cover: only about a third of currently serving consultants have signed the 2023 contract shown here — most remain on older contracts (2008 Type A/Type B, or the 1997 Revised Common Contract), which have a different structure (some permit private practice, category/region-dependent rates) and aren't charted here; see the source PDF's Consultant tab for those. And these are scale points, not a distribution of who actually earns what — this says what the bands are, not how many doctors sit at each point, or how allowances (on-call payments, up to €12,014/year on the most onerous rota; a €58,446 Clinical Directors' allowance; etc.) change take-home pay on top of base salary. Source: HSE "Health Sector Consolidated Salary Scales", effective 1 August 2025, data/raw/hse/hse-consolidated-salary-scales-aug-2025.pdf — Intern, SHO, Registrar, Specialist Registrar, and Senior Registrar scales (page ~4), Consultant Contract 2023 Clinical and Academic scales (page ~42).

07 Every grade, one chart — and it isn't a pyramid

All 15,043 hospital doctors publicly employed in Ireland in 2025, by grade, in one bar chart: Consultant down to Intern, with the Senior House Officer and Registrar grades split into their training-scheme and non-training-scheme (NTSD) halves — the same two populations charted separately in Sections 01-04. A textbook seniority pyramid narrows every step up. This doesn't: Consultant is the single largest grade in the whole system, bigger than any individual training rung below it. That's not an error — Consultant posts are long-tenure career endpoints doctors hold for decades, while Intern/SHO/Registrar posts rotate a much larger number of different doctors through the same seats every single year. A headcount snapshot isn't a measure of pipeline throughput.
